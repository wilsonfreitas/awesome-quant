# Choosing a backtesting tool

These tools model different research workflows; popularity does not tell you which assumptions fit your strategy. Choose the simulator whose data model and execution rules you can explain and validate.

| Tool | A good fit | Modeling limits and burden | Maintenance and support |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Backtesting.py](https://kernc.github.io/backtesting.py/doc/examples/Quick%20Start%20User%20Guide.html) | Learn a strategy loop or test entries and exits for one instrument using OHLCV bars. Strategy code runs through `init` and bar-by-bar `next`; the library supplies a compact broker, trades, statistics, and plots. | Bring a pandas OHLCV DataFrame. Its own guide says it is aimed at one tradable asset and does not really support stock picking or multi-asset portfolio rebalancing. Commission and spread can be configured, but bar data cannot reveal intrabar price paths or guarantee realistic fills. Its core stack is pandas, NumPy, and Bokeh. | Project documentation, source, issue tracker, and discussions live in the [official repository](https://github.com/kernc/backtesting.py). Check recent releases and unresolved issues for your use case. |
| [vectorbt (community `vectorbt`)](https://github.com/polakowo/vectorbt) | Explore signals across assets, parameter grids, and time series with pandas/NumPy-style arrays; then inspect simulated orders, trades, costs, and portfolio statistics. | You supply and align the data and signals. The community portfolio API models orders/signals with configurable fees, fixed fees, and percentage slippage; it is not automatically a bar-by-bar market microstructure model. The docs warn to execute after a signal, such as shifting close-derived signals forward. Requires the PyData stack and Numba; a Rust engine is optional. | Use the [official docs](https://vectorbt.dev/) and [GitHub repository](https://github.com/polakowo/vectorbt) for source, releases, and issues. The community `vectorbt` package is distinct from paid **VectorBT PRO**. PRO adds features such as additional data integrations, more order/path-dependent simulation, and cross-validation; do not assume [PRO examples or features](https://vectorbt.dev/getting-started/upgrade/) exist in the community package. Review the [license](https://github.com/polakowo/vectorbt/blob/master/LICENSE.md) for your intended use. |
| [Zipline-reloaded](https://zipline.ml4trading.io/) | Study an event-driven algorithm and portfolio/accounting workflow, including order lifecycle, trading calendars, data bundles, and Pipeline cross-sectional research. Its simulator supports configurable commission and slippage models. | More setup: ingest or register a bundle, manage asset metadata and adjustments, and install a larger dependency stack with compiled extensions. Results depend on the bundle's historical universe, corporate actions, and timestamps being sound. Commission/slippage models make execution assumptions explicit, but do not make fills realistic by themselves. | The [current project docs](https://zipline.ml4trading.io/) say Zipline-reloaded is maintained for bug, security, and compatibility fixes; new feature development has moved to ML4T Backtest and the docs recommend evaluating that for new projects. Source, releases, and issues are in the [official repository](https://github.com/stefan-jansen/zipline-reloaded). Treat it as a fit for learning or maintaining Zipline workflows, and evaluate that roadmap before starting fresh. |

## Validate any backtest

- **Lookahead:** Verify every feature and signal was available at its decision timestamp. Shift close-derived signals to a later executable price; check resampling labels, publication delays, and revised data.
- **Survivorship:** Build the historical universe as it was known then, including delisted assets and historical constituent changes. Current ticker lists can bias results.
- **Costs and fills:** Include venue/broker fees, spread, slippage, market impact where material, and order-size or liquidity limits. Stress assumptions; a configurable cost field is not evidence that its value is realistic.
- **Portfolio rules:** Check position sizing, cash, leverage, short availability, corporate actions, calendars, order timing, partial fills, and how simultaneous signals compete for capital.
- **Out-of-sample discipline:** Keep a final holdout untouched during strategy selection. Record every tried variant; use walk-forward or other time-aware validation and account for multiple testing.
- **Reproducibility:** Pin code and dependencies, save data source/version and universe, document assumptions and random seeds, and preserve the exact configuration and results.

These three existing catalogue projects were selected to contrast a single-instrument strategy loop, array-based research, and an event-driven portfolio workflow. They are examples, not a complete shortlist or a ranking. Revisit the guide when a relevant upstream change or a reported error affects these comparisons.

The linked project documentation and repositories were checked on **2026-11-14**. These references establish documented capabilities, stated limits, dependency expectations, and project-maintenance signals; they are not an installation or environment compatibility certification.
